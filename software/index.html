Python tool for cryptic binding pocket discovery in proteins, RNA and DNA. Generates conformational ensembles (NMA by default; optional OpenMM implicit-solvent MD and Boltz-2 diffusion sampling), detects pockets in every conformer with either a geometric alpha-sphere detector or a learned surface model, clusters them across the ensemble, and ranks them with a learned model over 23 geometric and ensemble-derived features, with an optional PLM-assisted ranker. Evaluated with a size-robust (Jaccard ≥ 0.25) protocol on four independent datasets. On CryptoBench's designated test fold, the zero-dependency default recovers 56% of cryptic sites in its top five and 66% with the PLM-assisted ranker; pooling the geometric and learned-surface candidates via --detector surface-fusion reaches 74%, lifting coverage from 69% to 86% (paired +16.8 points, 95% CI +11.4 to +22.8). Elsewhere: 73% (default) and 80% (PLM-assisted) on PocketMiner, 45% and 41% on a curated apo/holo set, and 87% on COACH420 general binding sites, rising to 97% with --detector surface-fusion. CryptoBench follows the dataset's own homology-separated folds, with the ranker's coefficients fitted on the train folds only. It also scores every residue: on the same held-out fold (231 targets, 76,633 residues), the per-residue propensity reaches AUROC 0.774 and AUPRC 0.345, against 0.697 and 0.249 from the input structure alone, and beats a burial-only baseline by +0.229 AUPRC (95% CI +0.199 to +0.261). On RNA sites with no detectable cavity in the input structure, the ensemble raises the fraction found from 44% to 73%. Outputs docking-ready Boltz YAML constraints, AutoDock Vina configs, pseudoatom PDB files with hotspot-centered pocket localization, the full conformational ensemble as a multi-model PDB, and a flag for pockets that sit at a chain interface. Ships an MCP server and a Claude skill, so the repository imports directly into Claude Science. MIT licensed; v1.4.0 on PyPI as lacuna-pockets. Paper: doi:10.64898/2026.08.14.744956; accompanying analysis: doi:10.64898/2026.08.11.743381.

vCell
Validation leaderboard: rank 292 of 1,654 teams (top 17.7%)

My entry in the Arc Institute's 2026 Virtual Cell Challenge, competing as Team Silver Labs. The task is zero-shot: given the unperturbed state of a cell line and a gene knocked down with CRISPRi, predict how thousands of genes respond in cell contexts with no perturbation data, scored against Perturb-seq measurements the model has never seen. The approach is deliberately constrained to one person, one desktop GPU, and only openly licensed data, to test how far a carefully built model can go against systems trained on much larger private datasets. On the frozen validation leaderboard, scored on a 300-perturbation panel across three cell contexts, vCell ranked 292nd of 1,654 teams (top 17.7%) with an overall score of 0.1996. Featured in Arc's 2026 competitor showcase. The code and a record of negative results will be released after the challenge closes.

ProteinTensor

AI-native biomolecular storage format (.ptt) for structural biology ML. Converts a protein, nucleic-acid, or protein-ligand structure - or a raw sequence - once and caches every training tensor: atomic coordinates, backbone geometry, bond graphs, MSA alignments, ESM2/ESM3 embeddings, dense or sparse pair features, and ligands with binding-site pockets - in a single Zarr/LZ4 container with memory-mapped lazy access and S3/GCS streaming. Generates native inputs for Boltz-2 (verified end-to-end), AlphaFold 3, Chai-1, OpenFold, and Nesso. On a 76-3,525 residue benchmark: 2-95x faster full loads, up to ~250x backbone-only loads vs re-parsing mmCIF, ~4x faster full feature assembly, and up to ~75x smaller pair storage via sparse radius graphs. PyTorch / JAX / NumPy.
